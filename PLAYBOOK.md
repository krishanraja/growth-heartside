Source: the two documents Krish gave this engine on 14 October 2026, kept in
`knowledge/` ("The Organic Pet Commerce Engine" and "Organic Pet Commerce
Growth Corpus"). They were written for a generic UK pet-accessories store.
This file is how the engine applies them to Heartside. `knowledge/README.md`
lists every place where Heartside's canon or Krish's rules change them.
How copy and offers are written and reviewed comes from a third source, the
Copywriting, Offers and Demand Corpus (19 October 2026), applied in
`knowledge/COPY-DOCTRINE.md`.

## The goal

Krish, 14 October 2026: "solely focused on getting this business to 10k a
month with minimal paid CAC", and "how it can become a dominant force in what
it does globally".

- **The number.** $10,000 of net sales in one calendar month, then every
  month after. Shopify is the ledger. Platform numbers explain attention.
  They never stand in for orders.
- **The first window is Christmas.** In heartside's canon
  (`docs/POSITIONING.md` section 6, read live each run), the target is about
  $10,000 by the 10 December order-by date. The checkpoints are 15 orders by
  1 November, 80 by 22 November and 220 by 10 December. If the canon changes
  them, the canon wins.
- **After Christmas,** $10,000 a month comes from occasions and evergreen
  search (see "The calendar").
- **Then everywhere else** (see "Becoming the category leader").
- **Minimal paid.** Agents never spend. Heartside's plan allows two paid
  moves, each needing Krish's yes for that spend. One is about $10 a day of
  retargeting site visitors from mid-November. The other is boosting the
  best creator post. Krish's wider rule caps paid tests at $500 a month
  across all his products, and they start only once owned channels bring
  revenue. The weekly report may propose a spend with its evidence. It never
  assumes one.

## The one idea

**"This year, the dog's buying."** (V3 section 8.) A dog gets the person it
loves a gift. A 90-second chat with Buddy turns what the buyer knows about
the two of them into words. Those words go on a mug, a blanket, a print or an
ornament, signed "Love, [dog]" with a paw. Nobody else in the category can
show words that came from the buyer's own answers. Every piece of content
shows that idea. Every link goes to the chat.

The documents' operating principle, made Heartside's:

> One idea. One Reel a day that a person would send to someone. Native
> editions for each platform. Every link tracked to an order. Every winner
> multiplied within 24 hours. Every leak in the store reported within 24
> hours.

## The equation

    net sales = qualified sessions x store conversion x average order value

The documents' scenario table, redone with Heartside's prices (V3 section 3:
mug $29, blanket $89, framed print $89, ornament $24, or $19 with another
gift; approved by Krish on 13 October, V3 decision log). **These are
arithmetic, not a forecast.** The
conversion rates are assumptions to replace with the store's own numbers.

| Basket | Order value | Orders for $10,000 | Sessions at 1.5 in 100 converting | Sessions at 2.5 in 100 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mug alone | $29 | 345 | 23,000 (767 a day over 30 days) | 13,800 (460 a day) |
| Mug and ornament | $48 | 209 | 13,930 (464 a day) | 8,360 (279 a day) |
| Blanket or print alone | $89 | 113 | 7,530 (251 a day) | 4,520 (151 a day) |
| Blanket or print and ornament | $108 | 93 | 6,200 (207 a day) | 3,720 (124 a day) |

What follows from it:
- **The order value matters as much as reach.** Moving the typical basket
  from a lone mug to a mug and an ornament cuts the orders needed from 345 to
  209. The choose-gifts screen (several gifts ticked, the ornament at $19
  with another gift) is a growth lever. Report on it every week.
- **Judge by contribution, not revenue.** V3 section 3 gives the margin
  after Printful and shipping, before Teeinblue and tax: the mug about $13,
  the blanket about $36, the print about $39, the ornament about $9 to $10.
  A blanket order is worth about three mug orders.
- **One more limit the documents call fulfillment.** Every order is checked
  by a person before it prints (HANDOFF rule 9), in a twice-daily order check
  Krish approved on 13 October (V3 decision log). At 8 to 10 orders a day,
  that check is Krish's time. Count it in the report. Flag it before it
  becomes the bottleneck.

## The content system

**The editorial brief (internal, not copy).** The account is about the bond
between a dog and the person it loves most: waiting at the door, the side of
the bed, the morning walk, the couch. It should be worth watching even for
someone who never buys. Business headlines stay plain, warm and true (V3 2).
The lead line is "Your dog loves you too."

**The chat is the content.** Each of Buddy's questions (V3 4.2) is a
comment prompt, a series and a source of buyers' own words:

| Chat question | Series and comment prompt |
|---|---|
| "Who's your dog?" | "Comment your dog's name" (the comment loop, the 12 Oct plan) |
| "Who does [dog] love most?" | "Send this to the person your dog loves most" |
| "What does [dog] do when [person] comes home?" | "What does your dog do when you come home?" |
| "Where's [dog]'s spot when [person]'s around?" | "Whose side of the bed is it now?" |
| "What do [dog] and [person] do together?" | "Morning walk, couch or car rides?" |
| "What is [dog] to [person]?" | "Shadow, best friend or the boss of the house?" |

## Measurement

**Shopify is the revenue ledger.** Platform analytics explain attention.
They never overrule orders.

**Creative ids.** Every draft that leads somewhere gets one:

    {PLATFORM}_{PILLAR}_{FORMAT}_C{concept}_H{hook}_V{version}_{YYYYMMDD}
    e.g. IG_EMO_REEL_C03_H07_V2_20261019

- **Platform:** IG, FB, TT, YT, PIN, GG (Google), EM (email), WEB, CR (creator).
- **Pillar:** EMO, ENT, UTIL, PROOF, OFFER.
- **Format:** REEL, STORY, POST, CAROUSEL, PIN, SHORT, VIDEO, LIVE, REPLY, BIO, LISTING, ARTICLE, EMAIL, NOTE.

Concept numbers run on through the whole engine. The same concept keeps its
number across platforms.

**Every tracked link.** Build it with `python3 growth.py link <url>
<creative_id>`:

    ?utm_source=<platform>&utm_medium=<organic_video|organic_social|organic_search|product_listing|email>
     &utm_campaign=hs_growth_<YYYYMM>&utm_content=<creative_id>

Shopify's marketing reports group sessions, orders and sales by these tags.
The checker refuses an untracked heartside.io link.

**The creative record** is now `local/engine.sqlite3`, kept across runs on
the same machine with portable backups (`docs/ENGINE.md`). The old
`local/creatives.csv`, if present, is legacy history. Record: creative_id, concept, pillar, format,
hook, promise, proof, call to action, search phrase, source assets, rights,
landing url, approval (posted or skipped), published url, and the 24-hour,
72-hour and 7-day reach, sends, saves, profile visits, link taps, sessions,
add-to-carts and orders, then the learning and the next action.

**What counts, in order:**
1. Contribution margin by creative.
2. Orders per 1,000 reached.
3. Sessions per 1,000 reached.
4. Chat starts, chat finishes, gifts added to cart (the theme's step events, V3 11.7).
5. Sends per reach (the judge for the A/B test), then saves.
6. Profile visits and link taps per 1,000 reached.
7. Watch time and completion.
8. Likes and follows.

A video with 5,000 views and five orders beats one with 500,000 views and none.

**Funnel diagnosis** (the documents' table, on Heartside's journey). Name
the weakest link in the persuasion chain for each row before proposing a fix
(`knowledge/COPY-DOCTRINE.md`, section 7):

| What the numbers show | Likely cause | Next test |
|---|---|---|
| Low reach, good orders per reach | The hook or packaging | Ten new openings on the same body |
| High reach, few profile visits or link taps | Entertaining but no reason to act | Show the gift and the chat sooner |
| Link taps but few chat starts | The landing page or the in-app browser | Check the page inside Instagram's browser; one clear "Start with your dog" |
| Chat starts but few finishes | A step that stalls | Find the step in the events; report it to a heartside build session |
| Finishes but few carts | The gift choice, the price or trust | Clearer gift choice, the order-by date, "a real person checks every order" |
| Carts but few orders | Shipping surprise, checkout or trust | A mobile test order; check every cost shows before checkout |
| Orders with a small basket | The choose-gifts screen | Report the multi-gift rate; propose a copy test |
| Orders waiting to print | The human check is the bottleneck | Count the queue; tell Krish before a promise is missed |

**Decisions.** Each creative gets one call at 24 and 72 hours, measured
against the platform's own rolling 14-day median: SCALE, ITERATE, HOLD or KILL.
- **A winner:** brings orders at a margin worth having, or brings sessions
  per 1,000 reached at least one and a half times that median, with enough
  views to trust.
- **Scale within 24 hours:** keep the original as it is and write its
  mechanism in one sentence. Make five new hooks, three audience or occasion
  versions (partner, mom, dad), a carousel, a Pin and a proof block for the
  store page.
- **Kill:** a concept after three clearly different hooks fail to move the
  signal it was meant to move, unless it brings search traffic or answers a
  real question.
- **The mix:** 70 in 100 drafts on what has worked, 20 on close variations,
  10 on new formats. Before the first week ends, "what has worked" means
  this account's own first week, not internet advice.
- **Test one thing at a time:** the same body with three hooks, the same
  video with two calls to action, the same concept for a partner and for a
  mom.

## Becoming the category leader

Krish asked for "a dominant force in what it does globally". In measurable
terms, being the leader means four things:
1. **Owning the phrase.** When someone searches or asks an AI assistant for
   "a gift from the dog", Heartside is the answer: Google, Pinterest, TikTok
   search and AI answers. Krish's AEO engine already checks AI answers each
   week for the ventures in Control Center. Adding Heartside there is his
   call. The report can propose it.
2. **Owning the season.** Heartside's share of US dog-gift buying around
   Christmas, Valentine's Day, Mother's Day and Father's Day.
3. **Repeat occasions.** A buyer who gave a mug at Christmas comes back for
   Mother's Day. Repeat customers are tracked from the first order.
4. **More markets,** once the US works.

**Stages, each with a gate:**

| Stage | When | Gate to move on |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Prove one repeatable content-to-order path in the US | Now to 10 Dec | At least one creative family brings orders again and again, attributed in Shopify |
| 2. $10,000 a month from occasions and evergreen search | Jan to May 2027 | Two calendar months at $10,000 with positive contribution |
| 3. New markets | After stage 2 | Evidence of demand from outside the US (Instagram's audience by country, buyers asking for shipping, search demand), and Printful printing locally in that market. Printful has a facility in Birmingham for the UK (the 12 Oct plan); check any other market in Printful before proposing it. **Changing markets is Krish's decision** (HANDOFF rule 2) |

**The moat to test: the share loop.** If each order brings visitors who also
order (through the share card, the recipient and sends), growth compounds
without spend. That is the metric worth owning: new sessions per order that
come from shares. Today the share card shows "heartside.io" as text, so
those visits can't be counted. The `conversion` job should propose a tracked
path for it to a heartside build session.

## What the engine never does

AGENTS.md has the full rules. In short: it never posts, sends, comments,
likes, follows, DMs, orders, confirms a Printful order, installs an app,
changes a setting or spends. It never invents a number, a review, a date or
a reaction. It never puts a customer's details in this public repository.
It never changes the heartside repository or the store. Fixes go to Krish as
drafts for a heartside build session.
