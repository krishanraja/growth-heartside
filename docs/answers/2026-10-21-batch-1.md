## 1. What can I get my partner from the dog?

Handle: `what-can-i-get-my-partner-from-the-dog` · Tier 1 · Leads to: any gift

**Short answer (excerpt)**

Get your partner something the dog would actually say. A gift "from the dog" works best when it names the dog's real habits: waiting by the door, stealing their side of the bed, the morning walk. Put those words on something they use every day, like a mug or a blanket, and sign it with the dog's name.

**Body**

The best gifts from the dog are specific. Your partner already knows the dog loves them. What makes them laugh, then go quiet, is seeing the dog's exact habits in writing, as if the dog had finally said it.

### Start with what the dog really does

Think about three moments:

- **When your partner comes home.** Does the dog wait at the door, bring a shoe, spin in circles, or play it cool for about four seconds?
- **The dog's spot.** On their feet, their side of the bed, next to them on the couch?
- **What the two of them do together.** The morning walk, coffee, movie night, naps, car rides.

Each one becomes a line in the dog's own voice:

> I hear your car long before anyone else.
> I took your side of the bed to keep it warm.
> Our walks are the best part of my day.

Let the dog's personality decide how it says them. A Border Collie keeps track of everything ("I keep the couch warm and one eye on you."); a Poodle has excellent taste ("I took the couch beside you because I have good taste."); a Beagle notices the world nose first ("The couch smells like you, so that's where I settle."). Same couch, three different dogs.

### Put it on something they'll use

- **A mug** if their mornings start with the dog and coffee.
- **A blanket** if the two of them own the couch.
- **A framed print** for a wall they walk past every day.
- **A tree ornament** for Christmas, every year.

### Sign it from the dog

End with the dog's name: "Love, Biscuit", with a paw print. It's the detail people point at.

## 2. How do you sign a gift from the dog?

Handle: `how-do-you-sign-a-gift-from-the-dog` · Tier 1 · Leads to: any gift

**Short answer (excerpt)**

Sign it the way the dog would: "Love," then the dog's name, with a paw print. "Love, Biscuit" reads warmer than "From Biscuit" and shorter than a full note. If the dog has a nickname everyone uses, use that. Keep the message above it in the dog's own voice, so the signature lands.

**Body**

The signature is the smallest part of a gift from the dog, and the part people remember. It turns a nice gift into one the dog gave.

### The simple version

- **"Love, [dog's name]"** with a paw print beside or under it. It works on cards, mugs, blankets and ornaments.
- Use the name the household actually says. "Love, Bartholomew Jr" is lovely; so is "Love, Bart".
- One dog, one signature. For two dogs on a card, "Love, Kuku and Mowgli" works; on a printed gift, keep the signature short.

### Make the words above it sound like the dog

The signature works best under a short message in the dog's voice: "I", the dog, talking to "you", the person.

> I'm your shadow, and I like it that way.
> Love, Kuku

A few lines that name real habits beat a long letter. Write each one as a real sentence the dog would say, in its own personality. Three lines is plenty on a mug; four suits a blanket or a framed print.

### What to avoid

- **"Dear [name]" letters.** They read like the owner wrote them. Let the dog get straight to the point.
- **Baby talk and "woof".** Dogs, it turns out, are very direct.
- **Anything sad.** A gift from the dog should make someone smile at breakfast.

## 3. What is a good Christmas gift from the dog?

Handle: `what-is-a-good-christmas-gift-from-the-dog` · Tier 2 · Leads to: the ornament

**Short answer (excerpt)**

A good Christmas gift from the dog is one the family brings out every year: a tree ornament with the dog's photo, name and one line in the dog's voice, signed "Love, [dog]". Pair it with something used daily, like a mug, so the dog's message shows up on December 25 and every morning after.

**Body**

This year, the dog's buying. Plenty of people already sign the dog's name on Christmas cards. A gift that carries the dog's own words goes one better.

### The ornament: the gift that comes back every year

A ceramic ornament with the dog's photo, name and one line from the dog, printed on both sides:

> I'm your shadow, and I like it that way.

It goes on the tree, then back in the box, then out again next December. For a family, one ornament per person from the dog becomes a tradition.

### Something for every day after Christmas

- **The Morning Mug** for whoever walks the dog before coffee.
- **The Couch Blanket** for the person the dog sleeps on.
- **The Framed Print** for grandparents who miss the dog between visits.

### Make the words true to the dog

The message works when it names what the dog really does, in the way that dog would say it:

> I wait by the door for you, weekends too.
> You have your coffee and I wait for toast.
