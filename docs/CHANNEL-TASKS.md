# Channel tasks: who runs which, and the weekly loop

The seven channel tasks are defined once in krishanraja/ai-harness
`contract/channel-tasks.md` (Krish, 2026-10-30). This file keeps only
Heartside's mapping and its loop. `crew/registry.json` says, for
each specialist and task, what that task accomplishes for Heartside on its
channels and the job that runs it. `growth.py --self-test` (`crew_errors`)
refuses an outcome under a sentence, the same outcome on two specialists, a task
in an unscheduled job, no leading signal, or a platform Heartside does not post
to (Instagram, TikTok and Facebook only, Krish, 2026-10-29).

The seven tasks are a starting set, not a template (Krish, 2026-10-30). Here
they all serve one outcome: a US dog owner buys a gift their dog "sends" to
someone it loves. So ideas come from gift moments, by buyer and occasion. Every
script ends on the real product. Trends count only when they can carry the
real design unchanged. This is unlike a publication, where ideas come from
published pieces.

The tasks serve the North Star (net sales from organic posts, trailing four
weeks, $0 of paid ads), never follower counts.

## The weekly loop

For each scheduled row use the operating instructions below, the [Drive map](DRIVE-MAP.md)
and the current **00 README for Codex** in the social library. The sheet owns the exact
caption and timing. These practices guide the next build; never use them to edit a locked post.

1. **Gap.** Open with `X of Y, U-n, short by Z` against this week's ladder
   ($200 for the week of 26 October, $728 for 2 November, $1,417 for 9
   November, $2,431 for 16 November), from `growth.py attribution`.
2. **Contribution.** Split the gap across Instagram, Facebook and TikTok by
   last week's attributed orders. While every channel reads $0, split it
   evenly and say that is an assumption.
3. **Leading signal.** Use the column above.
4. **Tasks.** Choose the week's concepts from the idea bank and the trend scan
   so each channel's signal moves, up to 5 posts a day per platform under
   concept approval. At U3 and above, volume rises to the policy's bounds.
5. **Review.** Read each post at 24 hours, 72 hours and 7 days against the
   channel's own median, keep or kill in the Sunday report, and record his
   declines and amendments where the next draft reads them. The check-in
   asks whether the plan is on track.

## Running the two kinds of post

**Gift-led posts** show the actual product and give someone a reason to visit its page.
**Dog POV and real-moment posts** earn a send to one recognizable person. Before building,
finish the sentence “I'd send this to ___ because ___.” If the answer is just “they like dogs,”
the idea needs work. Show the evidence first: what the dog actually did, then the inflated
human explanation. A workplace self-review works because watching the oven is a tiny job
described as a career achievement. A second caption explaining why that's funny spoils it.
The two kinds belong in one rotation; their success measures differ.

Keep existing locked slots. Take the next free slot on the approved platform and alternate
a send-led post with a gift-led post when possible. Do not displace a locked post to achieve
the alternation. Keep the existing US Eastern slots, stagger and five-post daily ceiling;
they are house scheduling choices, not universal best times. Do not attach a product slide,
hashtag, shop link or “tag a mate” to an approved standalone joke. Test a commercial
follow-up as its own creative with a matching destination and its own approval.

### Platform instructions

| Channel | What to build and check before posting | What to read and what to do with it |
|---|---|---|
| Instagram | Use a feed photo when the contrast fits in one screenshot. Zoom out to phone size: both halves and the punchline must survive the feed crop. Use a Reel when motion *is* the reveal, or a carousel when the swipe supplies a second payoff. Write for the person who receives the DM cold. For gift-led rows verify the approved bio destination; do not add it to an exact-caption meme. | Sends per 1,000 reached if shown; otherwise label the available share metric. Inspect non-follower reach and profile visits separately. If people send it but few visit, keep the joke and test the journey to the product on a separate post. |
| TikTok | Start on a dog already doing something legible: guarding dinner, ignoring a cue, or performing an unexpected skill. Show the result before the backstory. Use a ranking only when there are several permissioned moments and a reason to wait for first place. Watch once muted; check caption placement and sound rights. Use the locked track and caption. | Compare watch time, completion and shares per 1,000 views on similarly long posts. Early abandonment suggests testing the first shot; strong completion with few sends suggests testing a more recognizable situation. Verify a usable profile link before proposing a shop CTA. |
| Facebook | Publish from the Heartside Page. Show the rule and the dog visibly breaking it; family or work context should make sense to someone seeing a friend's share. Upload the approved original with legible text and a clear first frame. For a gift-led row, check the caption link lands on the pictured product. | Shares per 1,000 reached for humour; outbound clicks, landing visits and attributed orders for gift posts. High shares and low visits answer different questions. A new border or a caption on a copied clip alone is a poor originality strategy. |
| Pinterest | Prepare a vertical 2:3 Pin for a specific search: breed + occasion, a gift idea, or a useful photo tip. Preview on a phone. Use the words a searcher would type in the title and description, file to a relevant brand board, and deep-link to the precise item or instructions shown. A joke with no useful destination is a weak click test. | Compare saves/impressions with outbound clicks/impressions, then landing visits. Read at 7 and 28 days before retiring for low discovery; the first hour only checks delivery and link health. Search presence is evidence of discoverability, not demand volume. |
| Threads | Use one observation someone can answer with a story. For example, ask which word their household now has to spell in front of the dog. If using a photo, give it context in the text. Verify the Heartside identity; never substitute a personal account. Draft an interesting reply as a follow-up idea; obtain consent before reusing someone's story or image. | Count substantive replies, reposts and quotes per 1,000 views where shown; inspect examples for an actual conversation. Check link visits separately on an approved post with a reason to click. Do not send replies from this workflow. |

The first three are the existing factory channels. Pinterest and Threads are preparation
instructions until the engine supports them, a brand destination is verified and eligible
schedule rows exist. A connected account or a high owner rating is not a scheduled post.

### Publish once; learn from the result

1. Read the current library README and all listed schedules. Match the platform, brand
   identity, slot, approved cell, media, format, fingerprint and caption. Check the slot
   against both queued and live items; resolve an ambiguous provider receipt before retrying.
2. Only publish from a row Krish approved: `approved = yes`, or "Approved in chat" with the date in `notes` (Krish, 4 Nov). Verify the actual permalink and read back the
   media and words. A scheduler ID is not a live URL. Use only the permitted status,
   live_url, notes and Results fields as directed in the live README. If the asset requires
   a crop, caption replacement or changed audio, return it to the engine; do not improvise.
3. At one hour check delivery and the first signs of sends or clicks. Log reach/views,
   absolute actions, denominator, elapsed time and whether organic. At 24 hours, 72 hours
   and 7 days compare like format with like format on the same platform. Follow the live
   README's Sunday Results cadence. Mark unavailable as “not shown,” never zero.
4. Choose the next test by what failed. Good reach but few sends: change the social
   situation. Good sends but no profile visits: test an adjacent gift-led post. Clicks
   without visits: check the destination first. Visits without orders: inspect the offer
   and checkout. A cold account has no useful median at first; show counts and build the
   baseline. Never add platform views together as unique people or claim a shared bio
   link proves which post produced an order. Keep boosted traffic out of organic results.
5. Stock the library with release media only. Record whether the visual, performance and
   audio are owned or cleared for the intended channel and use. An original repost, a
   creator-native share, a commentary edit and a paid ad are separate uses. Keep source
   references, review renders and rejected versions out of Drive and public Git. Do not
   turn organic approval into a boost or assume a second channel inherits permission.

rankings had a 1.7M median against roughly 567K for six courtroom installments on the
same account. One Instagram trick Reel had 358K views against its account's sampled
12-Reel median of 12.45K. On Facebook a dog going off the agility course had 266K views
against a sampled median of 137.5K. On Threads one seasonal dog post attracted 573 replies.
Pinterest search surfaced relevant Pins, without comparable native performance counts.
