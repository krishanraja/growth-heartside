# Dog does dog things: scripts for the human-format reels (for Krish before any generation)

Krish, 10 Nov, on the held human-arms lip-syncs: "I think the gym era one needs to be funnier in that the dog would be
bench pressing a heavy log or doing sprints to chase a tennis ball, or more dog-like things. Its arms need to not be
human. In the same way, I think you need to upgrade most of these videos with what a dog would actually be doing.
That's the funny bit: a dog's Monday morning is likely to be very different from a human's Monday morning, but the
absurdity of it is that we show it as if it's a a human video - but its a dog, doing dog things." He also asked for a
new one: a dog's LinkedIn inspirational post, styled like an influencer. "These need to be really well scripted
before they go to video production, given that we've not got too many Higgsfield credits left." Floor entry:
`dog-does-dog-things`.

**North Star part it moves:** the organic sales number, through the sprint's lead viral franchise. Nothing moves until
a script is approved, made, judged and posted.

## The formula, every film

- **The frame is human, the dog is a dog.** Each film is shot and cut like a known human video format (gym montage,
  morning routine, day in the life, haul, Sunday reset, first-day vlog, LinkedIn grindset). Every beat fills one slot
  of that format with what a real dog actually does in it. The laugh is the gap between the slot and the behavior.
- **No human arms, no human behavior from the dog.** A real dog, real anatomy, doing real dog things with its mouth,
  paws and nose. A dog may wear what real dogs wear (a bandana, a knitted jumper, a headband for the gym). People
  appear only as an off-screen hand throwing a ball, feet, or a blurred figure.
- **Each beat pairs a sincere slot with a real dog behavior.** The slot is shown by the framing and, where the format
  uses them, by on-screen labels (see "Text" below). The behavior is what the dog visibly does.
- **Pace and ending.** A new beat every 1.2 to 2 s, a laugh every 3 to 4 s with the sound off, and a physical button
  at the end. One dog, one breed, per film, passed as the reference on every still.
- **Why this is cheaper:** dog-only motion is what Kling does best. Most of our retakes were third hands and pasted-on
  arms, which this format removes.

## Text on screen (your call)

The human formats these parody carry their own text: the morning-routine and LinkedIn formats stamp a time and an
activity ("4:00 AM, cold plunge"); the others carry a title only. Your no-lyric-captions ruling stands: no sung words
on screen, ever. **Proposal:** the LinkedIn film and the Monday film use format labels (a time and a two-word
activity) in a clean influencer style, because the label against the behavior is the joke. The other films keep the
premise line on shot 1 only. Say no and every film keeps the premise line only.
