What that says: the hook is failing. TikTok's 3.5-second average watch means most viewers leave before the

accounts follow a power law, so the realistic path is 3 to 6 posts that break out to 40,000 to 150,000 each,
plus a long tail of 1,000 to 3,000. The plan is built to get enough shots on goal for that and to pour the
next day's production into whatever breaks.

## 3. The creative doctrine for this sprint

**Proven formula, reskinned, with Heartside's twist.** Every post starts from something that already worked: a
format from Krish's playbook (200 top dog posts, 26 franchises, 130 concepts: Google Sheet
`1v3VgCjD8Zrh0WrxLNFisTQ1sGbgdUJfqRR97SmgYGa4`, in `factory/bank/franchises.json`), a meme template, a trending
edit or a track people already know. It is reskinned with our dog and our line and, on signature posts, ends on the
real product.

- **Reskin directly.** Proven memes, edits and backing tracks are reused, and a winner's frames can be references
  for generation (Krish, 10 Nov). Our own winners are reskinned without limit.
- **Every franchise is in play**, including the realism-stretch motion ones. Photoreal dogs a viewer cannot quite
  call real or generated.
- **New lead franchise: the human-arms lip-sync.** Krish's pick ("This is Good"): YouTube Short `u7s47cIeh6M`,
  "My dog's 9 to 5", 32 seconds, 18.8M views when read on 10 Nov. A shepherd in a hoodie with a hidden person's arms
  stages every lyric of a song everyone knows literally, one beat a second, with the lyrics on screen: tumbling out
  of bed, "pour myself a cup of ambition", the shower, traffic, glasses on at the laptop. It works because the
  premise is legible in frame 0, the song is already in everyone's head (so the comments quote it), a new gag lands
  every 1 to 2 seconds, and the illusion makes people rewatch. We make it as eight concepts (HAL-01 to HAL-08) on day
  one, including the signature post "My dog's Christmas shopping", which ends on the real mug.
- **The first second does the work**, with the sound off. That answers the 3.5-second average watch.
- **Design for the quote.** Quote and callback comments lead the playbook's comment data (TikTok: 1,692 sampled
  comments averaging 23,800 likes). Every post carries one line people will type back.
- **Krish's quality bar holds** (`python3 growth.py floor`): it is how the posts travel. Anything below 10/10 is
  remade, not shown.

## 5. How each post is made: the gates

One slot moves one gate at a time (`factory/sprint.py advance`). The stages are idea, script, script approved,
still, checked, shown, approved, built, locked, filed, row approved, live, and the 72-hour read.

1. **Script (Claude).** The line or voiceover, the first frame, the payoff and the comment prompt. Lines go into
   `factory/bank/lines.json` as proposed. **Gate: Krish**, on one page, in batches of 20 to 40.
2. **Still (Claude through Higgsfield).** 2 to 3 generations per shipped still. Each gets a realism record for
   the source and the crop, the caption-covered test and a score out of 10. Below 10 is held with its reason.
3. **Shown and approved (Krish).** One review page a day, best first. His yes on a concept and its exact mock is
   concept-level approval (AGENTS.md, 29 Oct) for every post derived from it. A decline goes to
   `factory/floor.json` in his words, the same day.
4. **Motion (B and R only, on an approved still).** Shot list as set-up, turn and payoff. Runway or Higgsfield
   video from the approved still. Frame-checked at full speed and frame by frame. A still's approval never
   carries over to its motion.
5. **Build (Codex, by card).** One card per day's batch: the manifest lines are the posts, built by
   `factory/build.py` (`still`, `film`, `chat`, `search`). Claude re-runs the acceptance and looks at every
   output at full size before anything is filed. A fail goes back as a new card. Every card is recorded in
   `codex/INDEX.md`.
6. **Lock and file.** `factory/lock.py check`, `media`, `brief`, `record`, then `gate` with the placement review.
   `factory/send_library.py` files the locked media to 03b `Posts/<id>/`. Rows go into a new `02e` schedule
   Sheet with `approved` left blank for Krish.
7. **Row approved, live (Krish, then the publishing desk).** Krish marks `approved = yes`, and the publishing
   desk posts exactly that. The engine never posts.
8. **72-hour read (Claude).** Views per platform from Buffer analytics, recorded with `sprint.py views`. At 3
   times the median or more, the post fills the next double-down slots. Below half the median, the franchise
   gets no more variants. `sprint.py status` prints both.

**Krish's time.** Day 0 is about 30 minutes: this plan, then the first batch of premises. Each day after is
about 20 minutes: one page of that day's stills and mocks (approve or decline), then marking the 02e rows.
Production runs about one day ahead of posting, so batch N is approved on day N-1.
