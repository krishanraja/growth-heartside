What that says: the hook is failing. TikTok's 3.5-second average watch means most viewers leave before the
joke. 500,000 views from 120 posts is about 4,200 a post, about 47 times today's TikTok average. Views on new
accounts follow a power law, so the realistic path is 3 to 6 posts that break out to 40,000 to 150,000 each,
plus a long tail of 1,000 to 3,000. The plan is built to get enough shots on goal for that and to pour the
next day's production into whatever breaks.

## 3. The creative doctrine for this sprint

**Proven mechanism, Heartside's signature, an original twist.** Every post starts from a mechanism that worked
in Krish's playbook (200 top dog posts, 26 franchises, 130 concepts: Google Sheet
`1v3VgCjD8Zrh0WrxLNFisTQ1sGbgdUJfqRR97SmgYGa4`, imported as `factory/bank/franchises.json`). It is then made
Heartside's way: the dog's inner voice, genre parody, a line people quote back. It finishes with one twist that
makes it ours.

- **The re-stage rule.** Proven posts inform; they are never inputs. We copy a winner's mechanism, timing,
  framing and beat structure, described in words. We never feed another creator's image, video or audio to a
  generator, trace or restyle it, or reuse their dialogue or shot-for-shot edit. A copyright strike on a new
  account mid-sprint ends the sprint. Heartside's own winners are reskinned without limit: that is the
  double-down lane.
- **Realism stretch, on Krish's 10 Nov go.** Photoreal dogs doing things a viewer cannot quite call real or
  fake. The bar is the floor: `real-dogs` (anatomy, fur, scale, light) and `real-behavior-in-motion` (every
  movement has a cause). Ten franchises the first draft excluded on quality grounds come back as grade R. Three
  stay out because they would pass fiction off as a real animal's record: dated rescue-progress stories, talk-
  button "evidence" and recognition "tests".
- **Platform AI labels stay on.** TikTok and Meta require realistic generated media to be labelled. The label
  does not kill the "is this real?" reaction, and an unlabelled post risks removal.
- **The first second does the work.** The visual problem is on screen in frame one, readable with the sound
  off (the playbook's own rule). It answers the 3.5-second watch.
- **Design for the quote.** In the playbook's comment data, quote and callback comments dominate: on TikTok,
  1,692 sampled comments averaging 23,800 likes. Each post carries one line people will type back, and its
  first comment asks them to extend it.
- **Krish's floor applies to every post** (`python3 growth.py floor`): `dumb-stopping`, `stage-the-joke`,
  `troll-not-mild`, `text-clear-of-faces`, `real-product`, `no-badges`, `ten-out-of-ten`, `funny-bar`,
  `retire-weak-premise`. Retired premises stay retired: the rain scene, Operation Stocking, the F06 Frenchie
  roast lines, the OG-01 to OG-05 executions.

## 5. How each post is made: the gates

One slot moves one gate at a time (`factory/sprint.py advance`). The stages are idea, script, script approved,
still, checked, shown, approved, built, locked, filed, row approved, live, and the 72-hour read.

1. **Script (Claude).** The line or voiceover, the first frame, the payoff and the comment prompt. Lines go into
   `factory/bank/lines.json` as proposed. **Gate: Krish**, on one page, in batches of 20 to 40.
2. **Still (Claude through Higgsfield).** 2 to 3 generations per shipped still. Each gets a realism record for
   the source and the crop, the caption-covered test and a score out of 10. Below 10 is held with its reason.
3. **Shown and approved (Krish).** One review page a day, best first. His yes on a concept and its exact mock is
   concept-level approval (AGENTS.md, 29 Oct) for every post derived from it. A decline goes to
   `factory/floor.json` in his words, the same day.
4. **Motion (B and R only, on an approved still).** Shot list as set-up, turn and payoff. Runway or Higgsfield
   video from the approved still. Frame-checked at full speed and frame by frame. A still's approval never
   carries over to its motion.
5. **Build (Codex, by card).** One card per day's batch: the manifest lines are the posts, built by
   `factory/build.py` (`still`, `film`, `chat`, `search`). Claude re-runs the acceptance and looks at every
   output at full size before anything is filed. A fail goes back as a new card. Every card is recorded in
   `codex/INDEX.md`.
6. **Lock and file.** `factory/lock.py check`, `media`, `brief`, `record`, then `gate` with the placement review.
   `factory/send_library.py` files the locked media to 03b `Posts/<id>/`. Rows go into a new `02e` schedule
   Sheet with `approved` left blank for Krish.
7. **Row approved, live (Krish, then the publishing desk).** Krish marks `approved = yes`, and the publishing
   desk posts exactly that. The engine never posts.
8. **72-hour read (Claude).** Views per platform from Buffer analytics, recorded with `sprint.py views`. At 3
   times the median or more, the post fills the next double-down slots. Below half the median, the franchise
   gets no more variants. `sprint.py status` prints both.

**Krish's time.** Day 0 is about 30 minutes: this plan, then the first batch of premises. Each day after is
about 20 minutes: one page of that day's stills and mocks (approve or decline), then marking the 02e rows.
Production runs about one day ahead of posting, so batch N is approved on day N-1.

## 10. Counterpoints

- **Volume against the 10/10 bar.** 120 posts in 9 days at Krish's bar is the main risk to the brand. The plan
  holds anything below 10 rather than filling a slot. A short day beats a weak post, and the count may land
  under 120.
- **Generated realism ages badly.** If audiences start calling posts out as AI, the R lane stops on the first
  wave of those comments, and the A lane moves to licensed real photography.
