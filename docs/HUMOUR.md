## How it applies to Heartside

The profile sets the comedy. Heartside's rules still bind (AGENTS.md, rule 5, and the floor), so the cruelty lands
where the brand allows it:

| The profile wants | Where it lands at Heartside |
|---|---|
| Deadpan absurdism, played completely straight | Dogs treat a ridiculous plan as normal business: a security briefing, a budget meeting, a hostage situation over a blanket. Nobody winks |
| Affectionate cruelty, "you absolute bastard" | Dogs roast each other without mercy (vanity, cowardice, stupidity, greed). Gift lines may tease the person's habits and life with the dog, never their body, looks, age, money, job or family |
| Intellectual stupidity | Serious formats (corporate memos, court, security ops, research) run by dogs, executed with real knowledge of the format and gloriously dumb logic |
| Escalation with comic logic | Every post climbs: each beat is bigger than the last, and each step follows from the one before. Random is not funny |
| Puncturing pretension | The most self-important dog gets taken down by his own behavior (the shepherd and his face blanket) |
| Surprise over setups | No predictable puns, no stock meme lines, no "who's a good boy". The payoff should catch him off guard, slightly inappropriate within the rules |

**The test for every piece:** an intelligent observation, an utterly stupid premise, and a slightly cruel payoff. If
any of the three is missing, it is not done.

**The limits the profile names itself:** don't push past the funniest point looking for one more escalation, and
don't make three conceptual jumps the viewer can't follow in the first second.

Public copy keeps US spelling and no swearing; the profile's own swearing describes his taste, not our captions.

## The profile, as Krish supplied it

# Krish’s Humour Profile

I'd describe your humour as **dry, irreverent, intellectually mischievous, and increasingly absurd, with a strong appetite for taking the piss.**

There's a distinctly British-Australian sensibility to it, but also something quite specific to you: you enjoy the collision between intelligence, stupidity, and social discomfort.

Based on our conversations, I'd break it down into six characteristics.

## 1. Deadpan absurdism

You like taking an already ridiculous situation and making it progressively more ridiculous, while treating the whole thing as perfectly normal.

Your requests to make someone comically tiny, swap a goat and a human so the goat is holding the person, or put people into a police lineup are good examples.

The visual incongruity does the work. You don't need a punchline explaining why it's funny.

## 2. Affectionate cruelty

You enjoy a good roast, particularly when it exposes someone's vanity, incompetence, or ridiculousness.

There's an element of deliberate meanness, but I suspect the enjoyment comes more from the precision of the insult than from actually hurting someone.

**You like a joke that makes someone think, "You absolute bastard," while laughing.**

## 3. Intellectual stupidity

This is probably your most distinctive characteristic.

You seem to enjoy being deliberately stupid in a way that requires some intelligence to construct.

There's a particular pleasure in taking something sophisticated, technical, or socially serious and reducing it to something gloriously idiotic.

You understand the rules well enough to know exactly how to violate them.

## 4. Escalation and disproportion

You rarely seem satisfied with the first level of a joke.

You often push for it to be more extreme, more ridiculous, more cutting, or more unexpected.

The joke gets funnier to you as the response becomes increasingly disproportionate to the original situation.

But there's a limit: you don't seem particularly impressed by randomness for its own sake. The escalation still needs an underlying comic logic.

## 5. Social observation and puncturing pretension

I suspect you're particularly amused by the gap between how people present themselves and what they're actually doing.

Corporate self-importance, performative expertise, exaggerated confidence, and people taking themselves far too seriously are probably fertile territory for you.

You seem to enjoy humour that makes the ridiculousness of human behaviour suddenly obvious.

## 6. A preference for surprise over conventional jokes

You don't seem especially interested in standard setups and punchlines.

You've repeatedly rejected jokes that were technically competent but predictable, particularly when asking for football-related wordplay.

You want something that catches you off guard, ideally with a slightly inappropriate twist.

## Your likely comedy DNA

These are stylistic comparisons rather than claims about your favourite comedians.

- **Ricky Gervais / The Office:** The pleasure of watching someone's self-importance collapse under the weight of their own behaviour.
- **Larry David:** Social absurdity, petty grievances and taking an entirely unreasonable position with absolute conviction.
- **Chris Morris:** Intelligent, transgressive absurdity delivered with a completely straight face.
- **Matt Berry:** Commitment to ridiculous premises, exaggerated characters and an almost theatrical disregard for normality.

## Where I think your humour can fall flat

I suspect you occasionally find something much funnier than the people around you do.

Your mind may already have made three conceptual jumps while everyone else is still processing the first one.

There's also a potential tension between your enjoyment of a brutally accurate observation and another person's willingness to be the subject of it.

And because you have a low tolerance for predictable humour, you might push a joke past the point where it was funniest, looking for another escalation.

Those are hypotheses, rather than things I can confidently establish from our chats.

## My one-sentence description

**You have the humour of someone who understands exactly how the world is supposed to work and derives enormous pleasure from watching it behave like a complete fucking idiot.**

I'd also make one distinction: I have more evidence of what you *find funny* and what you *ask me to create* than how you actually deliver humour in person.

But my strongest impression is that your ideal joke combines three things: **an intelligent observation, an utterly stupid premise, and a slightly cruel payoff.**
